## At a glance
**Role.** UX research intern.
**Timeline.** Summer 2024.
**Products.** [MarAds](https://voice.botnoi.ai/marAds), an AI-powered text-to-speech tool for content creation, and [AI Voicebot](https://voice.botnoi.ai), which handles customer service across chat and voice calls.
**Methods.** Task-based usability tests with think-aloud and ratings, surveys, and interviews.

## What I did
I ran usability studies on both products. I also wrote an AI-UX research handbook that lays out the methods step by step, so the practice could continue without me. Five UX interns used it to run their own studies, and I mentored them while they did.

In a separate set of workshops, I walked cross-functional teams through who a feature could help or harm, and which values it touched.

## What the summer taught me

### A research practice is more than a set of studies
With two products and one summer, “what should we test?” was only half the question. The other half was how the team would keep learning after I left, which is why the handbook mattered as much as any single study.

### Codifying methods exposed my judgment calls
Turning habits into steps someone else could follow showed me which parts of research are method and which are judgment. The judgment calls were the hardest to hand over.

### Mentoring is its own discipline
Helping five interns run studies meant watching them make choices I would have made differently. I had to learn when to step in and when to let them work it out, and that is a big part of why I like teaching.

### Harm and benefit analysis depends on timing
The workshops on who a feature could help or harm grew out of my PhD work. In fast product conversations, the question landed best when I brought something the team could act on.
