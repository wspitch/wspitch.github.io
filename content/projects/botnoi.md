## At a glance
**Role.** UX research intern.
**Products.** [MarAds](https://voice.botnoi.ai/marAds), an AI-powered text-to-speech tool for content creation, and [AI Voicebot](https://voice.botnoi.ai), which handles customer service across chat and voice calls.
**Question.** How could 2 AI voice products get user insight on a routine basis?
**Method.** Task-based usability tests with think-aloud and ratings, surveys, interviews, an AI-UX research handbook, and mentoring 5 interns.
**Why a handbook.** Research capacity had to outlast an internship.
**Observed.** Teams wanted user insight but had no routine way to get it.
**Interpreted.** A research practice has to outlast one person, so the handbook mattered as much as any single study.
**Decided.** I presented insight-based recommendations to the teams, and they were implemented in the following rounds of design iteration and the product launch. Those updates were tied to a 28% increase in user base across the 2 products over 2 months. I also left a repeatable way to run AI-UX research.

## What I did
I ran usability studies on both products. I also wrote an AI-UX research handbook that lays out the methods step by step, so the practice could continue without me. 5 UX interns used it to run their own studies, and I mentored them while they did.

In a separate set of workshops, I walked cross-functional teams through who a feature could help or harm, and which values it touched.

## Impact
- **A 28% increase in user base across the 2 products**, over 2 months of update launches tied to the research I supported.
- The teams implemented my insight-based recommendations in later design iterations and the product launch.
- Usability studies on both products gave each team a first structured read on how users handled the product.
- The handbook gave Botnoi a repeatable way to run AI-UX research, and 5 interns used it to run their own studies.
- Cross-functional teams practiced asking who a feature could help or harm before building it.

## What the summer taught me

### A research practice is more than a set of studies
With 2 products and one summer, “what should we test?” was only half the question. The other half was how the team would keep learning after I left, which is why the handbook mattered as much as any single study.

### Codifying methods exposed my judgment calls
Turning habits into steps someone else could follow showed me which parts of research are method and which are judgment. The judgment calls were the hardest to hand over.

### Mentoring is its own discipline
Helping 5 interns run studies meant watching them make choices I would have made differently. I stepped in when a study’s validity was at risk and otherwise let interns learn from their own choices, and learning where that line sits is a big part of why I like teaching.

### Harm and benefit analysis depends on timing
The workshops on who a feature could help or harm grew out of my PhD work. In fast product conversations, the question landed best when it came while the team could still act on it, and when I brought an option alongside the concern.
