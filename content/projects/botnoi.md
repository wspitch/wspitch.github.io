## At a glance
**Role.** UX research intern.
**Timeline.** Summer 2024.
**Products.** MarAds, a text-to-speech tool for creators, and AI Voicebot, which handles customer service over chat and voice calls.
**Methods.** Task-based usability tests with think-aloud and ratings, surveys, and interviews.

## What I did
I ran usability studies on both products. I wrote an AI-UX research handbook to standardize the methods, so that research didn’t depend on me, and I mentored five UX interns who could run studies from it. I also ran workshops that walked cross-functional teams through who a feature could help or harm and which values it touched.

## What the summer taught me
**Building a way of working is different from running studies.** With two products and one summer, the useful question wasn’t only “what should we test?” but “how will this team keep learning after I leave?” That’s why the handbook mattered as much as any single study.

**Writing it down made me say what I usually do by instinct.** Turning my habits into steps someone else could follow showed me which parts of research are method and which are judgment, and the judgment parts were the hardest to hand over.

**Mentoring is a different skill from doing.** Helping five interns run their own studies meant watching them make choices I’d have made differently, and learning when to step in and when to let them find it. It’s a big part of why I like teaching.

**The harm question is easy to ask and hard to ask at the right time.** The workshops on who a feature could help or harm came out of my PhD work. Bringing that into fast product conversations taught me that the question lands best when it comes with something the team can act on.
