## At a glance
**Role.** UX research intern.
**Timeline.** Summer 2024.
**Products.** [MarAds](https://voice.botnoi.ai/marAds), an AI-powered text-to-speech tool for content creation, and [AI Voicebot](https://voice.botnoi.ai), which handles customer service across chat and voice calls.
**Methods.** Task-based usability tests with think-aloud and ratings, surveys, and interviews.

## What I did
I ran usability studies on both products. I also wrote an AI-UX research handbook that sets out the methods in steps, so research could carry on without me. Five UX interns used it to run their own studies, and I mentored them while they did.

In a separate set of workshops, I walked cross-functional teams through who a feature could help or harm, and which values it touched.

## What the summer taught me

### Building a way of working is different from running studies
With two products and one summer, “what should we test?” was only half the question. The other half was how the team would keep learning after I left, and that is why the handbook mattered as much as any single study.

### Writing things down exposed my instincts
Turning habits into steps someone else could follow showed me which parts of research are method and which are judgment. The judgment parts were the hardest to hand over.

### Mentoring is its own skill
Helping five interns run studies meant watching them make choices I’d have made differently. I had to learn when to step in and when to let them find it out themselves. It’s a big part of why I like teaching.

### The harm question needs good timing
The workshops on who a feature could help or harm came out of my PhD work. In fast product conversations, the question landed best when I brought something the team could act on.
