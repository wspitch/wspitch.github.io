## At a glance
**Role.** Facilitator and researcher.
**Team.** Three industry design professionals and 11 designers.
**Timeline.** April 2024.
**Methods.** Career roadmaps, team ideation, sketching, and Figma prototyping.
**The decision.** How to imagine AI-enabled design work beyond current web and mobile conventions.

## How the day worked
Three industry design professionals co-hosted with me, and we shaped the plan together. Eleven designers with at least two years of experience joined, in three teams balanced by experience.

![The design jam organizers and industry co-hosts posing together in front of an HCDE banner](assets/projects/design-jam/organizers.jpg)

We opened with a talk on AI trends, then a warm-up. Each designer sketched a roadmap of their career and marked where AI had already changed their practice, so everyone started from their own experience before imagining anything new.

Teams then picked a design space, sketched individually, chose one idea together, and prototyped it in Figma. The prompt invited them to break from web and mobile conventions. Each team presented its vision, features, value proposition, and anticipated impact, and we closed with a synthesis.

![A team presenting its Wonderboard concept on a projector screen](assets/projects/design-jam/presenting-wonderboard.jpg)

![A team presenting a sticky-note brainstorm of the pain points designers face](assets/projects/design-jam/presenting-brainstorm.jpg)

## What designers said
They told us they learned that AI should assist designers and not replace them, and that it helps to focus on one pain point. They valued the sketching, the prototyping, and the live input from the industry guests. And their biggest request was simply more: more time, more real examples, more conversation with the guests, even a roomier venue.

## How it fed my research
The jam doubled as a pilot for my designers study. I saw that 15 minutes was enough for rough ideation and sketching, so I set 20 minutes in the study to leave room to work with AI.

## Impact
- A published teaching approach at IEEE ProComm 2025, and a write-up in HCDE news, that other educators can adapt.
- The prompt and timing for my designers study.
- Eleven designers left with prototypes, with industry co-hosts giving live input along the way.

## What I’d do differently
The evidence here is participant feedback, so I call it a teaching approach, not a measured outcome. Next time I’d add a before-and-after measure.
